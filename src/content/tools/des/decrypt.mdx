faq:
  - q: >-
        The output is garbage. Is my key wrong?
    a: >-
        Often, yes — or the mode, or the IV, or the ciphertext was already decoded once. DES has no authentication, so a wrong key still produces 8-byte blocks. Switch the output to hex and see whether you recognise anything. If you do not, the key is not the one that encrypted this.
  - q: >-
        Does this handle parity bits?
    a: >-
        They are ignored, as the algorithm specifies. Paste the 8-byte key as you have it.
  - q: >-
        Single DES or Triple DES?
    a: >-
        If the key is 8 bytes, this page. If it is 16 or 24, the Triple DES page. A 16-byte key used here is truncated in nobody's favour: it is rejected.

## Reading DES

The inverse of the encrypt page. CBC needs the IV that was used to encrypt;
ECB needs nothing but the key. There is no tag to check, which is the other
half of why DES is not used for anything that matters: you will always get
bytes out, and only you can tell whether they are the message.

If the plaintext was PKCS#7 padded, leave padding on PKCS#7 and a wrong key
will often throw. That is a hint, not a proof, and it is the shape of a padding
oracle. For a one-shot decryption of a blob you already have, it is merely
convenient.
