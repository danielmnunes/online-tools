description: >-
    Convert an identifier to camelCase — first word lowercase, the rest capitalised, no separator.
faq:
  - q: >-
        How are words split?
    a: >-
        On whitespace, punctuation, digits, and camelCase boundaries already in the input. `XMLHttpRequest` is three words (XML, Http, Request); `hello_world` is two; `v2Draft` is three.
  - q: >-
        What happens to an acronym?
    a: >-
        A run of capitals followed by a capital-then-lowercase splits before the last capital, so `XMLHttp` becomes `xmlHttp`, not `xMLHttp`. That is the convention change-case and friends use.
  - q: >-
        First word?
    a: >-
        Lowercase, even if it was an acronym: `XML_HTTP` becomes `xmlHttp`. PascalCase is the page that capitalises every word.
  - q: >-
        Will `hello_world` round-trip?
    a: >-
        Through snake_case, yes: camelCase of `hello_world` is `helloWorld`, and snake_case of that is `hello_world`.

## First word low, the rest capitalised

The shape of a JavaScript variable and of a JSON field in most APIs. Words are split on the usual identifier boundaries, then joined with no separator.

The other six conventions of the same input sit under the result, because the usual next question after "make this camelCase" is "and snake_case?".
