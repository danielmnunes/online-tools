faq:
  - q: >-
        PKCS#1 or PSS?
    a: >-
        PSS is the better construction (random salt, proven in the random-oracle model). PKCS#1 v1.5 is what JWTs, many TLS certificates historically, and a great deal of existing data use. If you are signing something new that you control both ends of, prefer PSS. If you are matching a verifier, use what it asks for.
  - q: >-
        Is the message hashed first?
    a: >-
        Yes, inside the signature scheme, with the hash you pick. You do not pre-hash unless the protocol says the signature is over a digest you already have — in which case paste that digest as hex.

## RSA signatures

RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5 or RSA-PSS, computed by the Web Crypto API. The private key
is PKCS#8 or a JWK. The output is one modulus wide and is not DER-wrapped.

PSS uses a salt the length of the hash, which is what RFC 8017 and JWA agree
on. A verifier that expects a different salt length will reject a correct key.
